# Numeric Requirements for Nitrites/Nitrates in Cured Meat Products

## Minimum Levels
The Canadian Standards of Identity Volume 7 define "cured" as requiring "at least 100 p.p.m. of sodium
nitrite, potassium nitrite, sodium nitrate or potassium nitrate" in edible meat products.

## Maximum Levels
According to the Food and Drug Regulations:
- Standard products (non-bacon): "maximum input level of sodium nitrite salts is 20 g per 100 kg of
  meat product, i.e. 200 mg/kg or 200 ppm"
- Side bacon: "maximum input level of sodium nitrite salts is 12 g per 100 kg of pork bellies, i.e.
  120 ppm"
- Dry rub cured products on racks (exception): "62 g of sodium nitrite salts and 186 g of nitrate salts
  per 100 kg of meat product"

## Key Regulatory Requirements
"Cured" meat products must contain "salt and at least 100 p.p.m. of sodium nitrite, potassium nitrite,
sodium nitrate or potassium nitrate, or any combination of them."

## Maximum Allowable Levels
- Standard products: Maximum 200 ppm (20g per 100kg)
- Side bacon: Maximum 120 ppm (12g per 100kg)
- Dry rub cured products: Up to 62g nitrite salts and 186g nitrate salts per 100kg

## Approved Curing Methods
Standard approaches include slow curing (nitrates converted by bacteria) and rapid curing (nitrites
directly). Alternative methods permit cultured celery powder (preformed nitrites) or non-converted
vegetable juice powders with starter cultures; validation required for heat-treated products.

## Compliance Note
Bulk nitrite/nitrate salts must be "kept secure and account for their use in a record log."
