## Full citation

Tornberg, E. (2005). "Effects of heat on meat proteins – Implications on structure and quality of meat

## Abstract (as retrieved)

"The paper compares how globular and fibrous proteins respond structurally to heating, with globular
proteins expanding and fibrous proteins contracting. It describes meat protein composition and examines
thermal behavior of various meat proteins."

## Key quantitative findings (the collagen mechanism the app needs)

These are the numbers `00-SOURCE-MAP.md` §3.2/§10 flags as the reason this source is in the minimal-19 set
— the independent, peer-reviewed mechanism behind collagen denaturation and gelatinization, which is what
explains (but does not by itself settle) the 94°C-vs-57°C brisket disagreement (see the dedicated section
of the final report):

- **Collagen denaturation: 53–63°C.** "At temperatures between 53 and 63°C the collagen denaturation
  occurs, followed by collagen fibre shrinkage."
- **Fate after denaturation:** "If the collagen fibres are not stabilised by heat-resistant intermolecular
  bonds, it dissolves and forms gelatine on further heating." — i.e. the 53–63°C event is denaturation and
  shrinkage; conversion of collagen to gelatin (solubilization) is a SEPARATE, slower, higher/longer
  process, not bounded to 53–63°C by this abstract alone.
- **Sarcoplasmic proteins:** aggregate 40–60°C ("for some of them the coagulation can extend up to 90°C").
- **Myofibrillar proteins (in solution):** unfolding starts 30–32°C, protein–protein association 36–40°C,
  gelation 45–50°C.
